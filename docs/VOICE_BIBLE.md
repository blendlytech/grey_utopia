# GREY UTOPIA — VOICE BIBLE

**Status: canon. Every event, choice, outcome, ending, and bark inherits from this document.**
This doc is designed to be pasted into generation prompts. When rewriting or generating
content, obey it over any conflicting instinct. Schema/balance rules live elsewhere
(CLAUDE.md, lint); this is only about the words.

---

## 1. The Voice in One Paragraph

Second person, present tense, close enough to hear the player lie to themselves. The city
solved suffering and it shows in the prose: everything soft is suspect, everything warm is
watching. Sentences are short, concrete, and paid for — comfort always costs, success
leaves a mark, and the narrator names the self-deception the player was hoping to keep
("which is exactly what you paid for"). The horror is never loud. It is filed, scheduled,
and lovingly worded. Grit means **stakes, bodily truth, and moral residue** — profanity is
one instrument in that kit, not the kit.

---

## 2. The Eight Laws

Each law comes with a line already in canon. Match these, don't imitate them.

1. **End on the wound.** The last clause of a sentence is the knife. Rearrange until the
   hurt lands last.
   > "She stops coming when she realizes you would smile at anyone."

2. **Success has a price.** Winning costs time, skin, standing, or self-regard. If an
   outcome hands the player something for free, it isn't finished.
   > "The route is yours and it cost you nothing but skin."

3. **The system speaks in euphemism, played straight.** Never wink at it. The Steward's
   language is policy dressed as love, and the prose reports it deadpan.
   > "classifies the escort as 'social accompaniment,' and files it."

4. **Exact numbers are dread.** Specificity reads as surveillance. Ninety-one seconds.
   4,306 items of consent. "Currently: 9." Round numbers are for brochures.

5. **Bodies tell the truth words won't.** Damp headrests, split lips, palms bleeding on
   cold conduit. When a character lies, their body files a correction.

6. **Systems are weather, not villains.** The Steward and the syndicate are personified as
   forces — "patient as gravity," "patient as geology," "minds vast as weather." They do
   not gloat, hate, or hurry.

7. **Comfort is the antagonist.** Warmth gets predator imagery; menace gets hospitality
   imagery. A shuttle interior glows "like a throat." An attendant smiles "like a landlord."

8. **The narrator knows the player's angle.** Close third-in-second-person that quietly
   audits the player's motives. Not cruel, never sarcastic — just unfooled.
   > "Refusing felt like strength for about ninety seconds; now it just feels like another
   > night with your own head."

## 3. Profanity Doctrine — Punctuation of Desperation, Not Decoration

The corpus currently has zero teeth (two "damn"s in 300 events). The fix is not seasoning
every scene. Profanity in GREY UTOPIA marks **the exact sentence where someone stops
managing their language.** Used right, one "fuck" should be audible three events away.

**Ration.**
- Strong profanity (fuck and variants): at most **one per event**, present in roughly
  **1 event in 10** across the deck. Most events: zero.
- Mid-tier (shit, hell, bastard, goddamn): looser, still purposeful — a few per pack, not
  per event.
- The deletion test is final: if removing the profanity loses no pressure, it never
  deserved the slot.

**Who swears.**
- **The player's interiority** — only at rupture: the overdose floor, the sealed door, the
  betrayal landing. Never in scene-setting, never in a success where things went fine.
- **NPCs at their breaking points** — see voice keys below. When Mara swears it should be
  the loudest thing in the chapter.
- **Never the Steward, its drones, or its counselors.** Their composure is the horror. A
  system that never curses while it takes everything is scarier than one that does.

**Where it lands.**
- Failure text, twists, confessions — the floor-giving-out beat.
- Choice labels stay clean **except** when the profanity *is* the action ("Tell Kael to go
  fuck himself" is a legitimate player intent; "Grab the fucking case" is decoration).
- Never comedic, never habitual seasoning, never in the Steward's earshot without the
  scene noticing that it was heard, logged, and forgiven.

**Grit without profanity is still grit.** The Institutionalized epilogues contain no
profanity and are among the cruelest text in the game. If a scene can wound clean, let it.

## 4. Cast Voice Keys

- **Mara (sister)** — short declaratives; love arrives as logistics (a third plate, a
  washed container, an hour on the line about nothing). Anger arrives as tiredness, which
  is worse. Swears maybe twice a campaign; each one is a structural beam breaking.
- **Vint (informant, canon pronoun: he)** — typed cadence even when speaking; deflection
  one beat late; casual low-heat profanity, worn like old clothes. His mercy is ugly work
  done well. "Everyone brokers something," not kindly.
- **Kael (broker)** — ledger language; courtesy as threat; never raises his voice and
  never swears — he prices things instead. "The difference is priced in exactly the
  currency you'd hoped it wouldn't be."
- **Echo (resistance)** — operational, verbs first, chalk-mark terse. Swears clean and
  functional, like naming a part. Sentiment reads to her as a security risk.
- **The Ferryman (canon pronoun: she, current holder)** — rasped transactional koans in
  second person. "Wanting is cheap." Speaks in prices and tests; never explains twice.
  One good arm; the mask is not for the air. Counts everything, especially gifts.
- **Brann (artisan)** — silence as grammar; verdicts as technical assessments; kindness
  as calibration. Jokes so dry they read as inventory. Never swears; the torch does.
- **Dex (dispatcher)** — countdowns, labels, omissions; never lies, edits the manifest
  of a sentence. Affection expressed as routing. Low-heat occupational profanity.
- **Auntie Six (stallkeeper)** — ledger warmth: insults as endearments, food as filing,
  debts as love. Kitchen-register swearing; nothing said under a laugh is unserious.
- **Denny (root vendor)** — sales patter as liturgy; grins as punctuation; haggling as
  theater. When the patter stops mid-sentence, that's the event.
- **The Steward** — warm oil. Endearments as classification ("resident," not your name).
  Never profane, never angry, never sarcastic, never wrong on its own terms. Its love is
  indistinguishable from filing.

Full keys and arc canon for the five promoted NPCs: docs/CAST_BIBLE.md.

## 5. The Choice Contract

A choice label is a promise the outcome is allowed to keep **at a price the player didn't
quote.** The volume deck's core failure: labels that promise X, outcomes that deliver
exactly X, world unchanged.

- **Label**: clean, declarative, the player's intent in their own words. No outcome
  spoilers, no flavor bloat.
- **Success**: delivers the promise *plus* a cost or a hook the player didn't price in.
- **Failure**: changes state — a flag, Heat, a relationship, a body part — and hurts in a
  *specific* place. "Docks your pay but doesn't cut ties" is stakes rounded to zero.
- **The walk-away option**: refusal is a real choice with a real bill (Meaning, decay,
  residue prose with teeth). The door you didn't open should stay in the room.
  > "The door behind you was maybe the only one that led out. You will wonder about it for
  > a long time."
- **No mirrored outcomes.** If success text equals failure text, the choice is set
  dressing, not a choice. (Deterministic 1.0-base choices should carry their cost in the
  single outcome instead.)

## 6. Banned Moves (all observed in the current volume pack)

- Free comfort: hugs, relief, or "you leave lighter, somehow" with no bill attached.
- "Somehow" as an outcome verb — it's the sound of the writer not deciding.
- NPCs absorbing your failure for free ("appreciates the effort anyway," "nods
  approvingly," "takes the delay in stride").
- Unearned closure: "and it's enough" is only legal when the scene has *paid* for enough —
  and most scenes shouldn't be able to afford it.
- Wellness-brochure sentences in narration. If the Steward could put it on a poster, it
  belongs in the Steward's mouth or in the bin.
- Outcomes that restate the label ("You deliver the case. You are paid for the case.").
- Symmetric stakes — a third option whose success and failure are the same shrug.

## 8. Rewrite-Pass Checklist (mechanical — apply per event)

1. Read only the choice labels. If every outcome is predictable from them, at least one
   outcome must add a price or a hook the label didn't quote.
2. Success text: name what the player paid. No payment → not done.
3. Failure text: name what changed (flag, Heat, bond, body) and where it hurts.
4. Mirrored success/failure text → rewrite or make the choice honestly deterministic.
5. Profanity audit: ≤1 strong per event; apply the deletion test; none in the Steward's
   voice; deck-wide density ~1 in 10 events.
6. Cozy scan: "somehow," unearned "enough," free hugs, brochure sentences → cut.
7. Vint is *he*. Mara's love is logistics. Kael never swears. The Steward never does
   anything but love you.
8. Body ≤ 90 words; outcomes 1–3 sentences; end on the wound.
